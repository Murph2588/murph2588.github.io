What I do

I believe the people closest to the customer are usually best positioned to make AI adoption actually stick — not a separate AI team, not IT, the people who already know where the friction is. That's the practice this site is about: find where AI removes real friction, build it, and make sure the people around you can run it without you.

None of this started with AI, and it didn't start in customer success either. Eighteen years now, across four companies — I spent the first stretch carrying a bag in sales before moving into customer success, where I found I cared more about what happened after the contract was signed than the signature itself. The number I've carried through most of it: consistently landing 100-120% of quota, and net retention above 100% on every book of business I've owned. I've managed a small team directly, and led plenty of people who didn't report to me — the difference has mattered less to me than whether the group actually got better.

Four moves account for almost everything I've built this year. None of them are customer-success-specific — the systems change, but the moves are the same whether you're pointed at accounts, a pipeline, or a product backlog.

Right now that means leading customer success and platform implementation at a privacy/compliance SaaS company, where I own a book of 50+ enterprise accounts and ask one question constantly: does this make someone's job easier, or did we just add another login? It's also meant building the systems my own team runs on — the Record tab has the specifics.

Ship the capability, and most people find their own way eventually. Ship the know-how alongside it, and adoption stops being a project.

I try to take my own advice here: this year I've been going deep on agentic AI myself — Claude and Claude Code are what I reach for day to day when I'm building something, but I keep real hands-on time across the rest of the field too: ChatGPT for drafting and custom GPTs, Microsoft Copilot in both M365 and GitHub, Gemini inside Google Workspace, and Cursor and Replit for prototyping. The point isn't loyalty to one vendor — it's knowing which tool actually fits the job in front of you. The Notes tab is where I write that part down as it happens.

A few things I've shipped

AI Prompt Library, company-wide

Challenge

Reps and CSMs were relying on tribal knowledge to get good results out of the company's AI assistant — inconsistent prompts, no shared starting point.

Approach

Built an eleven-section prompt library by hand, organized by product area, then pushed to get it published as an official resource rather than a personal doc.

Outcome

Now published in the company's Help Center, referenced in customer webinars, and used by Customer Success, Sales, and Marketing teams company-wide.

Co-presenter, "Privacy in Practice"

Challenge

Customers wanted practical guidance on using AI in privacy work, not another theoretical webinar.

Approach

Co-designed and co-present a recurring session built entirely around real workflows — annual-lookback reporting, GDPR Article 30 gap analysis, AI and system-risk assessment.

Outcome

A standing customer touchpoint that keeps coming back by request, not obligation.

Cross-region migration in ~30 minutes

Challenge

A customer needed records moved between regions — normally days of manual mapping and validation.

Approach

Used AI-assisted tooling to handle the mapping and validation in one pass instead of by hand.

Outcome

Done in about 30 minutes, with data quality ahead of what the manual process typically delivered.

Full platform migration inside 30 days

Challenge

A new enterprise customer needed to be live fast, while a separate legacy migration of 200+ historical requests off a competitor's platform was still in progress.

Approach

Ran both in parallel — a tight, AI-assisted 30-day implementation plan for the new customer, and a structured multi-month plan for the historical backlog.

Outcome

New customer live in 30 days — one of 25+ AI-assisted, 30-day platform migrations off competitors' services delivered over the years; the historical migration completed cleanly over the following months.

Built an AI-driven re-engagement workflow

Challenge

Accounts going quiet weren't getting caught until renewal conversations were already tense.

Approach

Built a Salesforce-connected skill that flags accounts with no recent activity and drafts a tailored outreach for review.

Outcome

Adopted team-wide and cited by leadership as a model for AI's impact on the CS org.

Field Notes

Short, practitioner-level notes on building and shipping AI tools inside a customer success org — what worked, what didn't, and what I'd do differently.

Sep 2026

Not every quiet account needs the same play

The first version of the re-engagement tool I built did one thing: find a quiet account and hand back a tailored resource. It worked, but it flattened accounts that needed something else — a warm, specific reintroduction grounded in what actually happened on past calls, not a generic asset. The rebuild added that second play and let the CSM choose per account. What didn't change: it still only ever drafts the outreach, never sends it. CC'ing the account manager by default turned out to matter more to adoption than anything about the AI itself.

Sep 2026

Why I stopped writing prompts and started writing a playbook

A good prompt that lives in your head helps exactly one person: you. The AI Prompt Library only became useful company-wide once I stopped treating it as my own cheat sheet and started writing it for someone who'd never seen the tool before.

Aug 2026

Read before you talk to it

Before any conversation that actually matters, I stopped walking in on memory and gut feel. Now AI pulls together what's scattered across the systems I use into one short brief first. The part that matters most isn't the summary — it's telling it to flag when two sources disagree instead of quietly picking the more comfortable answer. That's caught more than one situation where the official status and the real status weren't the same thing.

Aug 2026

The best AI tool is the one your team already trusts

Our re-engagement workflow didn't get adopted because it was clever — it got adopted because it slotted into a habit people already had. The lesson: build the AI into the workflow, not a new workflow around the AI.

Jul 2026

Restart what's gone quiet before you guess why

When a relationship goes quiet, the instinct is to guess why and write an apology email. A better pattern: find what's actually gone quiet — cross-checked against real correspondence, not just what's logged — build something genuinely useful to lead with, and draft (never send) the outreach yourself. The best save I've had this way didn't come from guessing the reason correctly. It came from asking directly and getting a straight answer back.

Jul 2026

What eight Anthropic courses taught me that customer calls didn't

Working through Anthropic Academy's courses back-to-back this year, the biggest shift wasn't technical — it was realizing that "agentic" work means designing a process someone else can run, not just getting a good answer yourself once.

Jun 2026

One thread, not ten

None of my other habits with AI hold up if every conversation starts from zero. The fix wasn't a smarter prompt — it was running a real body of work through one continuing thread instead of a new chat every time something came up, so each step builds on the last instead of re-explaining the goal. Point that same habit at status instead of a task, and it turns into something that just checks in on what's decided or still open whenever I ask.
